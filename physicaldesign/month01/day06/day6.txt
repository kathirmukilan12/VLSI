#### 🧩 Boolean Truth-Table Generator

I also developed a significantly more advanced **Boolean expression truth-table generator**. 
Instead of hardcoding individual expressions, I created a tokenizer, parser, and evaluator that can process expressions containing **NOT, AND, and OR**. 
The parser follows the precedence:

```text
NOT → AND → OR
```

This allowed expressions such as:

```text
NOT (A AND B)
NOT (A OR B)
NOT NOT A
A AND (NOT B)
```

to be evaluated systematically for every possible combination of Boolean inputs. 
I also used recursive parsing in `parse_not()`, which allows expressions such as `NOT NOT A` to be handled correctly.

#### 🔍 Verification & De Morgan's Laws

I learned how Boolean algebra can be connected to **verification thinking**, which is fundamental in EDA. 
For De Morgan's laws, I worked with transformations such as:

```text
NOT (A AND B) ≡ (NOT A) OR (NOT B)

NOT (A OR B) ≡ (NOT A) AND (NOT B)
```

The important verification step is to generate the truth table for both the original and transformed expressions and compare their **RESULT columns row by row**. 
This demonstrates logical equivalence rather than simply assuming the algebraic transformation is correct.

#### 🧠 Parser Reasoning

While working on the truth-table generator, I also explored concepts beyond the basic assignment. 
I understood why `parse_not()` uses recursion — it allows multiple consecutive NOT operations such as `NOT NOT A`. 
I also examined why the parser's call hierarchy places the lower-precedence **OR** operation at the top, followed by **AND**, and then the higher-precedence **NOT** operation. 
This helped me understand operator precedence as an actual parsing mechanism rather than something to memorize.
