Day 7 — Logic Gates + Combinational Circuits

#### 🧠 Concepts Learned

On **Day 7**, I learned the physical meaning behind the Boolean algebra from Day 6 by studying the **seven basic logic gates**: AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR, XOR, and XNOR. 
I understood how Boolean expressions, truth tables, and gate-level circuits are three different representations of the same logical behavior. 
I also learned why **NAND and NOR are universal gates**, meaning any Boolean function can be constructed using only NAND gates or only NOR gates.

#### 🔧 `nand_universal.py` — NAND Universality

I created **`nand_universal.py`** to prove that NAND can be used to construct other fundamental gates. 
I implemented:

```text
NOT A = A NAND A
AND A,B = (A NAND B) NAND (A NAND B)
OR A,B = (A NAND A) NAND (B NAND B)
```

I then verified each NAND-based construction against the corresponding original gate using truth tables. 
All **3/3 NAND constructions passed verification**, confirming that the implementations produced the same outputs as NOT, AND, and OR.

#### ➕ `full_adder_verify.py` — Full-Adder Construction

I created **`full_adder_verify.py`** to build and verify a **full adder** using two half-adders and an OR gate. 
I tracked the internal signals:

```text
SUM1
CARRY1
SUM2
CARRY2
```

and composed them to produce the final:

```text
SUM
CARRY_OUT
```

I tested all **8 possible combinations** of the three inputs (`A`, `B`, and `CARRY_IN`). 
The implementation passed **8/8 cases**, confirming that the composed full-adder logic was correct.

#### 🔍 Verification Thinking

I continued the verification approach introduced on Day 6. 
Instead of assuming that my gate implementations and full-adder composition were correct, I generated and compared truth-table results. 
This reinforced the idea that a circuit can be represented mathematically and then **verified systematically against expected behavior**, 
which is an important concept for future RTL and EDA work.

#### 🏭 Hardware & Standard-Cell Understanding

I learned why NAND universality matters beyond Boolean mathematics. 
A small collection of reusable logic primitives can represent arbitrary Boolean logic, allowing synthesis tools to select implementations based on **area, timing, and power** rather than requiring a unique physical cell for every possible Boolean function. 
I also learned the deeper CMOS-level reason NAND and NOR are important: **NAND/NOR structures can be physically cheaper than constructing equivalent AND/OR logic**, while an AND or OR implementation can require an additional inverter stage.

#### 🧩 Parser Design Insight

I recognized an important limitation in my implementation: although I successfully created standalone NAND and full-adder functions, I did **not yet extend the Day 6 Boolean parser itself** to recognize `NAND`, `NOR`, `XOR`, and `XNOR` as operators. 
I understood why this matters: extending the parser creates a reusable expression-processing system instead of requiring a separate Python function for every new expression or operator. 
This is an important step toward eventually handling more complex **EDA tool languages and expressions**.

Day 7 connected my **Boolean algebra knowledge to actual digital-circuit structures**.

I also strengthened my understanding of verification, circuit composition, universal gates, CMOS implementation trade-offs, and the importance of building reusable parsers rather than one-off scripts.
